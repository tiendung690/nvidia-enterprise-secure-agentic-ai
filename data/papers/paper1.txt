Retrieval Augmented Generation (RAG) and Beyond: A Comprehensive Survey on How to Make your LLMs use External Data More Wisely

Date: 23 Sep 2024
Authors: Siyun Zhao, Yuqing Yang, Zilong Wang, Zhiyuan He, Luna K. Qiu, Lili Qiu
Affiliation: Microsoft Research Asia

ABSTRACT

Large language models (LLMs) augmented with external data have demonstrated remarkable capabilities in completing real-world tasks. External data bolsters the models' domain-specific expertise, improves temporal relevance, and diminishes incidences of hallucination, thereby enhancing both the controllability and interpretability of outputs. Techniques for integrating external data into LLMs, such as Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) and fine-tuning, are gaining increasing attention. Nonetheless, the effective deployment of data-augmented LLMs across various specialized fields presents substantial challenges, encompassing retrieving relevant data, accurately interpreting user intent, and fully harnessing the reasoning capabilities of LLMs for complex tasks. The authors of this survey propose that no single solution fits all data-augmented LLM applications. Underperformance often arises from failing to correctly identify the task's core focus or because the task inherently requires a blend of multiple capabilities. This survey proposes a RAG task categorization method, classifying user queries into four levels based on the required external data and the task's primary focus: explicit fact queries, implicit fact queries, interpretable rationale queries, and hidden rationale queries. The work defines these levels, summarizes the key challenges and most effective techniques, and discusses the three main forms of integrating external data—context, small model, and fine-tuning—highlighting their respective strengths and limitations. This survey aims to help readers thoroughly understand and decompose the data requirements and key bottlenecks in building LLM applications.

***

1 INTRODUCTION AND QUERY STRATIFICATION

LLMs demonstrate extensive world knowledge and sophisticated reasoning skills but face significant deployment challenges in specialized fields, including model hallucinations and misalignment with domain-specific knowledge. Incorporating domain-specific data, especially proprietary or on-premise data, is crucial for tailoring LLM applications. Data-augmented LLM applications offer advantages such as enhanced professionalism and timeliness, alignment with domain experts, reduction in model hallucination, and improved controllability and explainability. Challenges in constructing robust data-augmented LLM systems range from building data pipelines (e.g., data processing and indexing) to achieving complex intelligent reasoning, especially concerning varied data forms (e.g., time series, images) and the need for interpretability.

The survey stratifies queries based on complexity and required data interaction:
* **Level 1: Explicit Fact Queries (L1)** are the simplest, seeking explicit facts directly present in the given data without requiring additional reasoning.
* **Level 2: Implicit Fact Queries (L2)** ask about facts not immediately obvious, requiring common sense reasoning or basic logical deductions by combining information across multiple data segments.
* **Level 3: Interpretable Rationale Queries (L3)** demand comprehension and application of domain-specific rationales explicitly provided in external resources, typically outside a general LLM's pre-training phase.
* **Level 4: Hidden Rationale Queries (L4)** delve into complex reasoning where rationales are not explicitly documented but must be inferred from patterns and outcomes observed in external data, requiring the discovery and extraction of implicit reasoning chains.

Levels 1 and 2 focus on retrieving and synthesizing factual information, while Levels 3 and 4 focus on the LLM's capacity to learn and apply rationales.

***

2 EXPLICIT FACT QUERIES (L1) AND DATA RETRIEVAL ENHANCEMENT

L1 queries are directly answerable by accessing specific domain documents, with answers often in plain text and requiring minimal reasoning. The core challenge is correct data retrieval, making **RAG** the most common solution.

**Data Processing Enhancement**
* **Multi-modal Documents Parsing:** Addresses unstructured data like tables, charts, and videos. Approaches include converting multi-modal content into textual form (e.g., Table-to-Text) or leveraging multi-modal embedding techniques as soft prompts.
* **Chunking Optimization:** Segmenting long texts into "chunks" is necessary. Larger chunks preserve context but contain more noise. Strategies include fixed size, recursive, sliding window, paragraph-based, and semantic chunking.

**Data Retrieval Enhancement: Indexing and Vector Indexing**
Retrieval involves establishing data indexes, processing queries, matching, re-ranking, and evaluation.

* **Sparse Retrieval:** Indexes text segments using specific words. Techniques like **TF-IDF** and **BM25** identify representative keywords, but they can suffer retrieval losses due to the inability to recognize synonyms.
* **Dense Retrieval (Vector Indexing):** This approach maps text segments into a **dense vector space of features** using pre-trained or fine-tuned text encoders. **BERT-based encoders** are commonly fine-tuned as dense retrievers using methods like **DPR**, **ANCE**, **SimCSE**, and **TAS-B**. More recent advancements include **LLM-based dense retrieval** (e.g., **LLM2vec** and **Llama2Vec**), which modifies pre-trained LLMs to act as powerful embedders.
* **Hybrid Retrieval:** Combines elements of sparse and dense techniques to focus on both central themes and global features, enhancing precision.

**Query-Document Alignment**
This step aligns the query with document segments in the external data.
1.  **Traditional Alignment:** Maps both documents and the query into the same encoding space using dual encoders or query encoders.
2.  **Document Domain Alignment:** Generates synthetic documents/answers, then uses them to recall data, addressing the distribution gap (e.g., **HyDE**).
3.  **Query Domain Alignment:** Maps text segments into the query space by generating synthetic questions for each unit, then retrieves the corresponding text.

**Response Generation Enhancement**
Generating accurate responses requires determining if retrieved information is sufficient and handling conflicts between retrieved and internal knowledge. Supervised fine-tuning (SFT) is effective, particularly when training data is designed to mitigate misleading effects from irrelevant or noisy retrieved context.

***

3 IMPLICIT FACT QUERIES (L2) AND RATIONALE QUERIES (L3 & L4)

### Level 2: Implicit Fact Queries (L2)

L2 queries require gathering information from **multiple documents** and aggregating the results through common-sense reasoning, often involving statistical queries, descriptive analysis, or multi-hop reasoning. Challenges include managing **adaptive retrieval volumes** and coordinating **reasoning and retrieval** iteratively.

* **Iterative RAG (Multi-hop RAG):** Similar to multi-hop RAG, it dynamically controls multi-step retrieval. **Planning-based** methods (e.g., **ReAct**, **IRCoT**) use a Chain of Thought to guide the RAG pipeline. **Information Gap Filling** methods (e.g., **Self-RAG**) autonomously decide when to search and refine answers based on generated knowledge.
* **Graph/Tree Question Answering:** Graphs naturally express the relational structure among texts, making them suitable for synthesizing information from multiple references. Approaches include traditional **Knowledge Graphs** (nodes as entities) and **Data Chunk Graph/Tree** models (nodes as text chunks).
* **Natural Language to SQL Queries (NL2SQL):** Converts user queries to database queries for retrieving information from structured data, augmenting LLMs with valuable external data.

### Level 3: Interpretable Rationale Queries (L3)

L3 queries require comprehending and applying explicitly provided, domain-specific rationales. Rationales are presented as **Plain Texts** (e.g., guidelines, handbooks) or **Structured Instructions** (e.g., workflow diagrams, decision trees). The challenge is integrating these rationales in a comprehensible manner.

* **Prompt Tuning:** Used to enhance LLMs' adherence to specific rationales, overcoming high manual prompt optimization costs. Techniques like **TEMPERA** use reinforcement learning to discover optimal prompt configurations, and methods like **OPRO** leverage LLMs themselves for optimization.
* **CoT Prompting:** Chain-of-Thoughts and related methodologies are effective for complex rationales necessitating extended reasoning chains, which are distinct from L2's factual reasoning.

### Level 4: Hidden Rationale Queries (L4)

L4 queries are the most challenging, requiring inference and discovery of rationales that are implicit, non-trivial, and too numerous to exhaust, representing tacit domain expertise. Data sources include **In-domain Data** (historical Q&A, simulation results) and vast **Preliminary Knowledge** (axiomatic systems, empirical summaries). Challenges include **logical retrieval** based on congruence rather than similarity, and **data insufficiency**.

* **Offline Learning:** Extracts generalizable rules and guidelines from datasets offline to enhance reasoning. Methods include generating rationales (**STaR**), generalizing errors into principles (**LEAP**, **RICP**), and distilling a meta-buffer across tasks (**Buffer-of-Thought**).
* **In-Context Learning (ICL):** Uses retrieved examples to uncover hidden rationales, leveraging the LLM’s few-shot learning abilities. This requires strategies to select optimal, diverse, and representative examples to prevent distraction by irrelevant information.
* **Fine-tuning:** Addresses the challenge of lengthy logical chains and extensive prior knowledge by enabling LLMs to rapidly grasp new domain rationales, building on their pre-training foundation. Techniques like **Instruction Tuning** and parameter-efficient methods (**LoRA**, **Adapter tuning**) are used to reduce computational costs.

***

4 CONCLUSION: DATA INJECTION MECHANISMS

Choosing the appropriate technology requires an in-depth understanding of the task and the complexity level of its queries. Knowledge is injected into LLMs through three main forms, each with trade-offs:

1.  **Context:** Providing extracted domain data as context input.
    * *Strengths:* Better interpretability and stability.
    * *Limitations:* Constrained by the finite context window and risks information loss.

2.  **Small Model:** Training a specialized small model to guide the integration and routing of external information to the LLM.
    * *Strengths:* Reduced training times and capacity to assimilate considerable data.
    * *Limitations:* Efficacy depends on the small model's capabilities, potentially capping LLM performance and incurring additional training costs.

3.  **Fine-tuning:** Directly using external domain knowledge to fine-tune a general LLM into a domain-expert model.
    * *Strengths:* Utilizes large model capacities with extensive domain-specific data.
    * *Limitations:* Impact is highly dependent on data design; risks generating erroneous outputs, losing prior knowledge, or neglecting unencountered tasks. It is the most costly method.

In practical applications, a **routing pipeline** integrating multiple methodologies is often necessary to effectively handle the typical combination of diverse query types.
